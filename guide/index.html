Time study from a video, step by step

Video Time Study (VTS, vts-tool.com) is a free tool, in the browser, to time a workstation from a video. Nothing is uploaded: the video stays on your computer or your phone. No account.

Open Video Time StudyTry the example (fictional data)

1. Film the workstation

  • Phone held still, on a tripod or set down, landscape, without zooming.
  • Frame the hands and the whole workstation, with the parts and the places the operator walks to.
  • At least 10 full cycles, ideally a few more. 720p at 30 frames per second is enough.
  • iPhone: Settings > Camera > Formats > Most Compatible, and HDR Video off. Otherwise the site converts the video for you.
  • Tell the operators first: you film the workstation to make the work easier, not to judge people.

2. List the steps

  • In the order they are done, each with its type: operation, inspection, walk, wait, machine.
  • Codes 10, 20, 30 come by themselves. A list can be pasted from Excel (Code; Name; Type).
  • Quick start: give the number of operators, machines and steps, and rename them.

3. Time with one key

  • Play the video and press Enter when each step starts: the site records the time and moves to the next step.
  • Interruptions, out-of-view moments and anomalies are noted so that the cycle is not used for T1.
  • Periodic tasks (box change, cleaning) are noted with P and counted per part.

4. Read the results

  • Cycle times (a), waits (b) and work content (c) for each cycle.
  • T1: the lowest repeatable time, the median or the mean of the usable cycles (at most the first 20).
  • Precision at 95 %, the number of cycles needed for 5 %, output per hour.

5. Balance the line (Yamazumi)

  • Every operator and machine as a column against the takt time.
  • In a scenario, drag a step to another operator: bottleneck, output and line balance follow.
  • The current state stays as measured.

6. Layout and walking paths

  • Import a plan (picture, PDF or DXF) or draw the stations.
  • Say where each step is done: the walking paths (spaghetti diagram) and the distance per cycle come out.
  • Move the stations in a scenario, compare before and after, play the animation.

7. Compare and share

  • Compare / Reports: the scenarios side by side against the current state or another scenario, then one PDF report on the layout, the line balancing or both.
  • A3 PDF presentation of the layout, Excel workbook (with the plans and the simulation of a scenario), filled-in standard sheets.
